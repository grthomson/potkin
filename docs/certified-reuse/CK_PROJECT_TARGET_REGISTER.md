# CK comparative proof and program analysis target register

Updated 5 October 2026. This is the stable target register. It indexes the existing proof records rather than replacing them. Every research iteration should identify the targets it advances, the precise new statement, its evidence level, remaining hypotheses, and the next obligation. Only one or two targets should be active at once.

## Organizing purpose

Compare equipped sequent calculi and typed programming languages by what their translations preserve about typed independent decomposition, primitive-base support, reduction, and observable behavior. The practical purpose is certified modular transformation and reuse: decide which admitted components remain independent and opaque, which interfaces must be exposed, and what must be rechecked when a base, program component, or presentation changes.

CK is the decomposition algebra in this programme. Its coproduct records simultaneous detachable regions; typed punctured remainders record their interfaces; context substitution supplies the McBride/Faà di Bruno composition mechanism; guarded histories restrict decomposition to regions that stay independent; base-relative coactions select trusted provenance; the antipode gives a signed relative core. Semantic contracts supply the additional behavioral obligations. These structures answer different questions and must not be conflated.

The double-category organization is explicit in T-DBL: horizontal translations/context substitution, vertical base extension, and compatible squares. A strict algebraic double category of split-selected protected CK carriers, with measured interchange, is now PAPER PROVED in the comparative base-change note. The full proof-relevant double category of arbitrary logical translations and operational simulations remains open. Exactness of a square and exactness of its horizontal arrows are distinct conditions.

- REPORTED EXECUTABLE means a native POTKIN implementation and focused checks reported by the user's Codex run. The Windows repository has not been independently inspected in this turn.
- OPEN means a theorem or semantic instantiation still required. A finite fixture cannot discharge it.
- STANDARD means an existing mathematical tool used in the construction. Novelty is proposed for the integration and compatibility results, not for that tool itself.

Universal definitions, proofs and symbolic counterexamples are the primary evidence for mathematical targets. Independent agents challenge correctness, novelty and practical value separately. Finite checks may corroborate an implementation or find a counterexample; a count of passed cases is not a theorem milestone.

## Exact sequences with proved algebraic hypotheses

Work over a field k (for example Q after scalar extension of native integer sums). This does not require a Racket coefficient-carrier change. Let i:B→H and π:H→B be Hopf maps with πi=id. Give H the left B-action i(b)h and left coaction ρ=(π⊗id)Δ. Let

\[
K=H^{\operatorname{co}B},\qquad
\Pi(h)=\sum i(S_B\pi(h_{(1)}))h_{(2)},\qquad
J=i(B^+)H,\quad B^+=\ker\epsilon_B.
\]

### E1 Relative material and core sequence

The fundamental theorem of Hopf modules gives H≅B⊗K via b⊗k↦i(b)k. Under this identification Π=ε_B⊗id. Consequently

\[
0\longrightarrow J\longrightarrow H
\xrightarrow{\Pi}K\longrightarrow0
\]

is exact and split as vector spaces by K⊂H. It is also an exact sequence of B-modules when K is given the trivial action; that module sequence need not split. It is generally not a comodule/Hopf-module sequence: J need not be coaction-stable and Π need not be colinear. K's ambient coproduct need not land in K⊗K. A quotient Hopf structure, when available, must be distinguished from the inherited coproduct.

Interpretation: separate base-generated algebraic material from the signed independent core. Neither J nor K is a carrier of positive checked proofs.

In our commutative CK setting there is additionally a genuine Hopf-exact sequence

\[
k\longrightarrow B\xrightarrow{i}H
\xrightarrow{q}Q=H/J\longrightarrow k.
\]

Here Hopf-exact has its standard extension meaning: i is injective, q is surjective, ker q=i(B^+)H, and H^{co Q}=i(B), where H^{co Q} is defined by (id⊗q)Δh=h⊗1. It does not mean ordinary vector-space exactness at B.

Proof in this commutative split case: J is a Hopf ideal, Π is an algebra map with kernel J, and q|_K:K→Q is an algebra isomorphism. The inverse of the Hopf-module decomposition is Ψ(h)=Σπ(h_1)⊗Π(h_2). If h is q-coinvariant, applying π on the first tensor factor gives (π⊗q)Δh=π(h)⊗1. Applying (q|_K)^{-1} on the second factor yields Ψ(h)=π(h)⊗1, hence h=iπ(h). Conversely q i=ηε proves every i(b) is q-coinvariant. H≅B⊗K also makes H free, hence faithfully flat, over B. This proves the stated Hopf extension. Transporting Q's coproduct to K gives a Hopf structure that need not be K's inherited ambient coproduct.

### E2 Comparative connecting map

Take a second split pair i':B'→H',π':H'→B', with J',K',Π'. Suppose F:H→H' and α:B→B' are Hopf maps satisfying Fi=i'α. Coaction exactness π'F=απ is not assumed. Then F(J)⊆J'. Set

\[
F_J=F|_J,\qquad F_K=\Pi'F|_K,\qquad
\beta_F=(\mathrm{id}_{H'}-\Pi')F|_K:K\longrightarrow J'.
\]

Relative to H=J⊕K and H'=J'⊕K', F has the block form

\[
F=\begin{pmatrix}F_J&\beta_F\\0&F_K\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Thus Π'F=F_KΠ and the two E1 rows form a commutative diagram. The snake lemma yields the genuine six-term exact sequence

\[
0\to\ker F_J\to\ker F\to\ker F_K
\xrightarrow{\partial_F}\operatorname{coker}F_J
\to\operatorname{coker}F\to\operatorname{coker}F_K\to0.
\]

For k∈ker F_K its connecting map is

\[
\partial_F(k)=[\beta_F(k)]\in J'/F(J),
\qquad \Pi'F-F\Pi=-\beta_F\Pi.
\]

Proof: F preserves the material subspaces by Fi=i'α and α(B^+)⊂(B')^+. The Hopf-module decomposition supplies the two vector-space splittings. The displayed block formula gives the commuting quotient square. Lifting k through K⊂H gives F(k)=β_F(k) when F_K(k)=0, exactly the snake boundary. The core-defect identity follows by applying both sides to j+k. This proves every asserted algebraic statement without finite enumeration.

Interpretation: a source core class is lost as target core, but becomes target material not already accounted for by translated source material. The connecting sequence need not classify all coaction defects: β_F can be nonzero without a nonzero connecting map. The new comparative theorem proves that full core transport IS equivalent to selector/coaction exactness under the split, inclusion-preserving Hopf hypotheses used here. Scalar core statistics and arbitrary non-Hopf translations do not have that equivalence.

More precisely, for k∈ker F_K, ∂_F(k)=0 exactly when a source-material correction j∈J exists with F(k-j)=0. This is a relative lifting statement; attaching a judgment-level conservativity interpretation requires an additional semantic calibration.

Nonzero example: H=k[t] with t primitive, B=k,π=ε; H'=k[a] with a primitive, B'=H',π'=id; F(t)=a and α the scalar inclusion. Then J=0,K=H,J'=(a),K'=k, F_K=ε and β_F(t)=a. The boundary sends t to [a]≠0. This models reclassification of an independent history/module as trusted material. A one-vertex operation with identity semantics is still a primitive generator a, not the empty-forest algebra unit.

Status: E1 is standard Hopf-module theory applied to our split selector. E2 and its interpretation are a new paper derivation in this project, independently algebra-checked, not proof-assistant mechanized. A genuine calculus translation satisfying these hypotheses remains to be instantiated.

### E3 A conditional long exact sequence for protected extension

Fix a base coalgebra C and a genuine subcomodule P⊂X. For a C-comodule Y, applying Hom_C(-,Y) to 0→P→X→X/P→0 gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
0&\to\operatorname{Hom}_C(X/P,Y)
\to\operatorname{Hom}_C(X,Y)
\to\operatorname{Hom}_C(P,Y)\\
&\xrightarrow{\partial}\operatorname{Ext}^1_C(X/P,Y)
\to\operatorname{Ext}^1_C(X,Y)
\to\operatorname{Ext}^1_C(P,Y)\to\cdots .
\end{aligned}
\]

A prescribed coaction-respecting translation g:P→Y extends linearly and colinearly to X exactly when ∂g=0. Choose any linear extension f:X→Y. Its defect δ=ρ_Yf−(id⊗f)ρ_X vanishes on P and factors through X/P; the resulting cobar cocycle represents the connecting class, up to the sign convention. A different linear extension changes it by a coboundary.

This has a nonzero elementary instance: C=k[x],x primitive; X=span{1,x} with Δ-coaction; P=k1; Y=k with trivial coaction; g(1)=1. A colinear extension would require (id⊗f)Δx=1⊗f(x), but its left side is 1⊗f(x)+x⊗1. Thus no extension exists and the connecting class is nonzero. The class is represented by ±x in Ext^1_C(k,k); in characteristic zero Prim(k[x])=kx.

Important scope: if Y is the entire relative Hopf module H', the Hopf-module theorem makes it cofree as a base comodule, hence injective. All the positive-degree Ext groups above vanish and unrestricted linear colinear extension is always possible. A nontrivial project application needs a genuinely constrained semantic/observed target comodule or a specified correction complex. Also P must be coaction-stable; an arbitrary read footprint does not suffice. Colinear linear extension does not establish multiplicativity, checked typing, logical conservativity or operational soundness.

Status: standard long exact sequence and a checked paper example; project application open. Prioritize E2 before commissioning homological implementation. A generic coaction defect of a linear map is a coboundary in the unrestricted two-comodule Hom complex, so it cannot simply be advertised as a nonzero H^1 class.
