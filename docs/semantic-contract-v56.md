# Semantic contract: v56 kernel and integral CK Hopf algebra

This repository implements the static calculus-relative proof/context kernel described around Definitions 1.1--1.4, Proposition 1.5, the two root profiles, the address-indexed CK factorisation, compatible base extension, basis constructor deletion, and exact nullary-ground repair in the v56 manuscript. Above that kernel it implements finite integral sparse sums of proof forests, ordered forest tensors, the full multiplicative CK coproduct, its counit and vertex grading, the connected-graded antipode, and formal maps induced by exact-ID calculus revisions. The supplied PDF and TeX source are read-only mathematical references and are not part of the implementation.

## Implemented representation

- Formula contexts are canonical finite multisets of immutable formula data.
- A finite formula-signature descriptor may declare atom symbols and symbolic
  operators of fixed nonnegative arity. Its generated formula constructors
  return the same ordinary deeply immutable symbolic data consumed by
  `formula-datum?`; the descriptor validates declarations and boundaries but
  is not a second formula AST or a Hopf-provenance token.
- A sequent is a pair of formula contexts. A hypersequent is a nonempty finite multiset of sequents. Equal displayed components retain distinct, local 1-based occurrence indices.
- A concrete occurrence has a unique registry key, a disjoint kind/tag, opaque fully instantiated data, an ordered vector of whole-hypersequent premises, one whole-hypersequent conclusion, and optional opaque incidence data.
- A finite rule signature declares only fixed tag, kind, and arity
  decorations. Declaration macros derive those fields for fully instantiated
  concrete occurrences and validate every occurrence profile and boundary
  before returning an ordinary equipped calculus. They add no schemata,
  metavariables, substitution, unification, or on-demand occurrence creation.
- An equipped calculus is a finite immutable registry. Profile equality never grants admission: raw applications identify the exact registered occurrence key.
- Raw candidates use the Redex-friendly shared syntax `(puncture)` or `(app occurrence-key child ...)`. Checked nodes and typed holes are separate values whose constructors are private to the checker.
- Declarative derivation forms compile `_` and lexical concrete-occurrence
  bindings into that shared raw syntax, first requiring each cited occurrence
  to be the exact registry object selected by its ID. They always validate an
  explicitly declared whole root and never infer a conclusion, resolve a tag,
  or construct checked nodes directly.
- Validation reports structured errors and checks registry membership, exact arity, exact ordered premise boundaries, and an optional expected root boundary. Checked terms expose root boundary, completeness, and vertex count.
- Punctures have positive premise-slot addresses (with `()` reserved for the typed nodeless identity), and their requirements are read from retained parent occurrences. The premise telescope sorts these address/whole-boundary pairs lexicographically.
- A derived context interface retains the exact calculus snapshot, canonical
  telescope requirement word, and output boundary. Addressed composition
  accepts one replacement for every original telescope entry and canonicalises
  arbitrary association-list order before checked insertion. Backward
  refinement inserts one exact admitted corolla. A filling-point value stores
  one ordered tuple of complete exact-provenance fillers; evaluation delegates
  to checked filling, while extraction decides whether a complete candidate
  preserves every retained exact occurrence and captures only at the fixed
  punctures. It does not enumerate or search the filling fibre.
- A corolla is one admitted occurrence with every premise vacant. Typed insertion prefixes the inserted context's internal addresses, context composition fills the current telescope, and complete filling requires a complete proof at every entry. These operations recheck exact boundary and registry provenance; their validity never depends on a filler search.
- Calculus provenance is retained for rechecking but excluded from structural proof-presentation equality. `term-admitted-by?` is only an extensional registry-admission query; it does not establish provenance identity. Operational insertion, forest construction, multiplication, and carrier-sensitive lookup require the exact same registry object with `eq?`. Cross-revision use requires explicit checked lifting first.
- A compatible calculus revision is an immutable source registry, exact withdrawn-ID set, fresh addition set, and internally constructed target `(K0 - D) + A`. Revision equality and hashing include the exact `eq?` identities of both endpoint snapshots; structurally equal registries do not identify operational arrows. Every retained occurrence is reused exactly; profile mutation, substitution, renaming, and component reindexing are absent. Historical validity remains source-relative, while target liveness checks every vertex's full occurrence identity and reports every retired, unknown, or incompatible/redefined address. `identity-calculus-revision` is the exact arrow `K -> K`; by contrast, `make-calculus-revision K '() '()` deliberately constructs a fresh structurally equal target and remains an explicit rebase.
- A revision chain is a finite ordered path with an explicit exact source. Its consecutive target/source pairs must be `eq?`; the empty chain is the identity path at its source. Checked terms, open contexts, proof forests, and formal values act along a chain by applying its steps sequentially. Paths are intentionally not flattened: after one step withdraws an ID, a later step may legally add a new occurrence with that ID relative to its then-current source. A formal monomial killed at the earlier step remains zero while the zero's exact target provenance advances through all later steps, so temporal ID reuse never resurrects historical evidence.
- Checked lifting is explicit rebasing: a source-certified live term or open context is converted to shared raw syntax and revalidated under the target with its exact root boundary. This preserves its presentation, addresses, puncture requirements, and telescope while changing registry provenance. No unchecked cast is exposed.
- Basis-level constructor deletion maps a historical positive-vertex checked node or proof-forest monomial either to a target-lifted survivor or to an explicit algebraic-zero value carrying the historical source and every withdrawn occurrence. A naked typed `Box^G` returns a structured domain error because it is not a basis tree. Term offenses retain their tree address; forest offenses retain a structural factor, its local address, and the factor multiplicity without imposing an order on the commutative forest. One affected factor kills the whole monomial, while the empty forest survives as the target algebra unit. This diagnostic `algebraic-zero` remains distinct from additive zero; the formal revision map interprets it by omitting the affected support term.
- An automatic nonroot ground-repair plan exists only for a complete historical source proof whose every target-inactive occurrence is explicitly withdrawn, material, and nullary. Its inactive leaf addresses form the CK cut; the plan retains the complete source witness (aligned detached tuple, commutative forest, source remainder, and reconstruction data) and separately stores the exact same punctured presentation revalidated under the target. Positive-arity, logical, structural, incompatible, and non-prefix-free frontiers return structured scope errors before cut construction.
- Applying a plan takes complete, live proofs carrying the exact target registry, in lexicographic telescope order and with exact whole-hypersequent roots. Structural extraction succeeds precisely when a candidate has the plan's exact retained constructor identities and ordered premise structure, with arbitrary complete target proofs only at its punctures. Filling and extraction are inverse on this selected retained context, establishing the implemented product-of-fibres bijection; this is not a claim about every target proof with the same root.
- A one-vertex withdrawn ground uses a separate whole-proof replacement value retaining the historical source, target identity context `Box^G`, and telescope entry `((), G)`. This endpoint uses the same checked filling/extraction interface but is not a CK cut and does not change root-cut rejection or cut enumeration.
- A proof forest is a commutative finite multiset of positive-vertex checked connected terms carrying one exact equipped-calculus snapshot throughout. Multiplicity is retained. Its block-root profile remembers one hypersequent per connected factor, while its flattened root unions all displayed component occurrences. The empty forest has no `HSeq` flattened root, and a nodeless identity context is rejected as a factor. Forest construction never silently revalidates a foreign factor, and forest union rejects distinct registry snapshots even when they are structurally equal.
- A CK cut is a finite prefix-free set of existing nonroot vertex addresses in a complete or punctured positive-vertex derivation. A witness retains the sorted address/subtree tuple, its commutative forest image, and the punctured remainder. Empty cuts are included; root cuts are rejected because the whole-tree tensor endpoint is algebraic bookkeeping, not a cut. Witness reconstruction fills only the newly cut addresses, preserving any older punctures. Cut enumeration structurally generates a lazy sequence without scanning inadmissible subsets.
- An integral formal sum is one immutable, finite, normalized sparse value carrying an exact equipped-calculus snapshot and a positive tensor rank. Each support key is an immutable vector of proof forests of that rank, and each stored coefficient is an exact nonzero integer. Construction collects duplicate structural keys and removes zero coefficients; support access never exposes the mutable coefficient table used while normalizing.
- Rank one represents finite integral combinations of proof-forest basis elements. The formal additive zero is empty coefficient support tied to one exact calculus snapshot. The algebra unit is instead the nonzero basis element of that calculus's empty proof forest. A checked-node convenience injection first forms its singleton forest, so complete and punctured positive-vertex rooted terms are admitted while a naked typed `Box^G` cannot become a basis element.
- Rank-two and higher values retain tensor-coordinate order. Forest factors inside each coordinate remain commutative, but `(F,G)` is not identified with `(G,F)`. Addition, negation, subtraction, exact-integer scaling, and componentwise forest multiplication are normalized and require the same positive rank and the same exact calculus object. Coordinate expansion and contraction likewise preserve coordinate order and reject incompatible ranks, snapshots, or replacement results with structured algebra errors rather than treating incompatibility as zero.
- Formal-value operational identity includes exact calculus provenance even though checked terms and proof forests retain structural presentation equality. Consequently, formal zeros and otherwise identical-looking sums over distinct but structurally equal snapshots are different values and cannot be combined without explicit checked lifting and reconstruction in one selected snapshot.
- Forest multiplication in both the kernel and formal algebra is independent commutative juxtaposition. It is not context filling, assembly, Mix, witness reconstruction, Gentzen Cut, or any implicit structural proof rule. A connected proof of `S | T` remains distinct from a forest containing separate `S` and `T` proofs even when their flattened roots agree.
- For a connected positive-vertex term `t`, the collected coproduct adds `t tensor 1` once and converts every existing admissible cut witness, including the empty cut, into `P^c(t) tensor R^c(t)`. The empty witness therefore supplies `1 tensor t` exactly once. The right remainder is always a singleton proof forest containing its positive-vertex checked root, possibly with typed punctures; no root cut or bare context tensor coordinate is manufactured.
- The coproduct of the empty forest is `1 tensor 1`. A nonempty forest is handled multiplicatively over every expanded connected-factor occurrence, including repeated equal factors, and then the operation is extended linearly to rank-one integral sums. No additional forest endpoints are appended after this multiplication. Equal tensor presentations collect with integral multiplicity, intentionally forgetting witness addresses while the occurrence-level witness API continues to retain alignment.
- The public reduced coproduct is the literal formula `Delta(F) - F tensor 1 - 1 tensor F`; on the empty-forest unit it is therefore `-1 tensor 1`, while antipode recursion invokes it only on the positive-degree augmentation ideal. For a positive connected `G`-rooted term, the root coaction instead removes only `t tensor 1`, retains the empty-cut term `1 tensor t`, and checks every right tensor coordinate is a singleton exact-provenance retained context of root `G`.
- Final-corolla factorization is defined for complete connected proofs and retains the exact final occurrence, source proof, ordered immediate-child vector, its commutative premise-forest image, and the full-premise corolla. Recomposition uses the ordered vector, never the forest ordering. The one-vertex right-coordinate projection of the coproduct is checked against the premise forest tensor the final corolla, including the nullary empty-premise case.
- One-slot insertion sums checked insertion of one positive connected context into every exactly typed puncture of another and collects equal results integrally. Its coefficient pairing with the singleton-cut coproduct is occurrence-level insertion/cutting duality: singleton cutting enumerates exactly nonroot one-address cuts, without endpoints or multi-address cuts. This operation is neither forest multiplication nor a generic operad action, and `Box^G` remains outside the Hopf carrier.
- The counit of a formal sum is its coefficient at the empty-forest basis element. Coordinate contraction implements both ordered tensor counit laws. The vertex degree of a forest is the sum of `derivation-vertex-count` over every factor occurrence; punctures contribute no vertices inside their positive rooted factor, the unit has degree zero, and `Box^G` has no Hopf degree because it is outside the carrier. A zero sum has an empty support-degree set rather than an invented homogeneous degree.
- The antipode is computed recursively from the collected reduced coproduct and strict positive vertex degree, with a calculus-scoped cache local to each top-level computation. Every recursive product is commutative forest juxtaposition. The resulting formal convolution inverse is not an inverse derivation, a filler, a reconstruction, a repair plan, a proof reduction, or a normalization procedure.
- A formal revision map accepts any positive tensor rank over the exact source registry. A coordinate forest containing a withdrawn occurrence kills its whole ordered tensor term; otherwise every coordinate is explicitly checked and lifted into the exact target registry before normalized coefficients are rebuilt. For deletion-only revisions this is the executable quotient projection into `H_Z(K0-D)`. With fresh additions it is deletion followed by inclusion into `H_Z((K0-D)+A)` and need not be surjective. Addition-only revisions implement persistent inclusion. `formal-revision-map-chain` is their literal sequential action along a validated revision path, including empty and exact-identity paths. No explicit quotient-module datatype is constructed.
- Exact executable checks cover the endpoint formulas, linearity, multiplicativity, both counit laws, ordered rank-three coassociativity, degree conservation, both antipode convolution identities, carrier closure, exact registry provenance, and the algebra/coalgebra/counit/antipode laws of one-step formal revision maps and their finite path composites. A finite test-local generator exhausts locally admitted complete and punctured nullary/unary/binary terms through four vertices and small forests. These computations validate this implementation slice; they are not a formal proof for every finite equipped calculus.

Complete proofs alone are not closed under this coproduct: cutting a complete proof generally creates a positive-vertex punctured remainder. The implemented carrier therefore includes both complete and punctured rooted checked terms. Every computation on a finite input is finite, although the complete proof-forest algebra of a calculus may have infinite rank.

The proof-tree carrier is cut-hereditary because admission is currently local: constructors are finite fully instantiated occurrences, every occupied child has the exact ordered premise boundary, typed vacancies remain valid without a filler, and no proof-global normality, freshness, or liveness condition restricts membership. Replacing a subtree by its typed vacancy therefore preserves local admission, and detached subtrees remain admitted. This claim must be reassessed if incidence metadata or a future calculus introduces nonlocal admission conditions; incidence is presently trusted occurrence metadata rather than an independently validated global constraint.

## Equality and indexing

The implementation uses structural proof-presentation equality. Boundary multisets absorb exchange and preserve counts. Component indices are deterministic local presentation indices; a future representation may implement the manuscript's more general equality by coherent component reindexing. Premise slots and tree addresses remain ordered even when their boundaries are equal. Forest factors are instead collected commutatively with multiplicity. Structural equality never proves shared calculus provenance: operational forest, formal-sum, and tensor APIs separately require exact `eq?` registry identity.
